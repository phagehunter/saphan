Subject: Reusing the myMediCon text corpus in an open phrasebook app for health volunteers in Kanchanaburi

Dear Dr. Ye Kyaw Thu,

My name is Curtis Hoffmann. I work in biosecurity and science communication
(Operation Outbreak) and I am starting a small open-source project for village
health volunteers and migrant health volunteers in Kanchanaburi, Thailand, who
serve Burmese- and Mon-speaking patients.

The project is a free, offline phone app built around a library of short,
human-verified clinical phrases with audio in Thai, Burmese, and Mon. I found
your myMediCon text corpus (version 0.8) on GitHub and would like to use a
selection of its Burmese sentences, with attribution, under the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0
license you published it under. Every record in the app carries the corpus
name, the line number, and your citation. I want to make sure this use matches
your intent and to ask three things:

1. Is the speech corpus (myMediCon or mediTalk-mm-rdy) something we could use
   for research-only evaluation of on-device speech recognition for medical
   phrases? We would not redistribute it.
2. Do you know of anyone working on Mon-language medical text or speech? We
   have found no Mon machine translation anywhere and are planning to work with
   Mon speakers in Sangkhlaburi for human translation and recording.
3. Would you or the LU Lab be interested in reviewing the Burmese side of the
   phrase set, or in co-authoring a short write-up if the app is piloted?

The repository (scaffold stage, nothing deployed yet) is at
https://github.com/phagehunter/saphan. I would be glad to send a short summary
of the design if useful.

Thank you for publishing the corpus openly. It made this project possible.

Best regards,
Curtis Hoffmann
curtis@operationoutbreak.org
