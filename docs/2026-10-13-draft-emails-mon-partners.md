## 1. University of Computer Studies, Yangon: the 25,000-pair Mon–English corpus

To: Dr. Khin Mar Soe, University of Computer Studies, Yangon [find current address on the UCSY
site or via the paper's corresponding-author line]
Cc: Ei Ni Tar Tun, Yi Mon Shwe Sin (co-authors)
Subject: Request to use your Mon–English parallel corpus for a non-commercial health phrasebook

Dear Dr. Khin Mar Soe,

I read your 2025 paper in the IAENG International Journal of Computer Science, "Exploring
Bidirectional Mon-English Machine Translation with SMT, NMT and Fine-Tuned BART," with great
interest. As far as I can find, your 25,000-sentence Mon–English corpus is the only one of its
kind.

I am building a free, open-source phrasebook app for village health volunteers and migrant
health volunteers in Kanchanaburi, Thailand, who serve Burmese-, Mon-, and Karen-speaking
patients. Every phrase is verified by a human before a patient sees it; machine translation is
used only to draft text for reviewers. The project is non-commercial (code MIT, content
CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), led by me, with no commercial partner.

Would you be willing to share the corpus, or the health and emergency-services portion of it,
for this purpose? I would be glad to:
- cite the paper and your group in the app and repository,
- share back any Mon health phrases we produce under an open license,
- report evaluation results on your data, and
- discuss co-authorship if a pilot is written up.

If the corpus cannot be shared, I would still value a short conversation about Mon
segmentation and the custom syllable vocabulary you built.

With thanks and respect,

## 2. Independent Mon News Agency (IMNA): permission to align the Mon and Burmese archives

To: [editor@monnews.org or the contact listed at https://monnews.org/contact]
Subject: Permission request: using IMNA's Mon and Burmese articles to build a Mon health-translation tool

Dear IMNA editorial team,

Thank you for twenty-five years of Mon-language journalism. I am writing with a specific,
non-commercial request.

I lead a free, open-source project building a health phrasebook for volunteers who serve
Mon-speaking patients in Kanchanaburi, Thailand (Sangkhlaburi, Thong Pha Phum, Sai Yok). No
machine translation system in the world supports Mon. The best available path to change that
is to learn from text that exists in both Mon and Burmese, and IMNA's Mon and Burmese editions
often carry the same story on the same day.

I am asking for permission to:
1. download IMNA's Mon and Burmese articles from your public website and RSS feeds,
2. align matching sentences by computer, and
3. use the aligned pairs only to train a Mon–Burmese translation model for health use.

I would not republish your articles, would credit IMNA in the project, and would share the
resulting model and any Mon health phrases back with you under an open license. I would also
welcome an IMNA journalist as a paid or credited reviewer of the Mon phrases, if that is of
interest.

Please tell me if there are conditions you would want, or if a different arrangement suits
you better.

With appreciation,
Curtis Hoffmann
curtis@operationoutbreak.org
Project page: https://github.com/phagehunter/saphan
